# 01. Game design

Status: draft 0.2, 2026-11-13. Includes the author's answers of 2026-11-13. Tags like (D6) point to [09-decisions.md](09-decisions.md), which says whether each choice is decided, proposed or open.

## Title

**La vie des crevettes**. In English: **The Life of Shrimp** (D33).

## One line

You live one life as a shrimp, from a drifting larva to an adult in the open sea, and you feel what it feels along the way.

## Purpose

Help the general public see shrimp differently: as animals that feel and deserve respect. A player should finish with a good image of shrimp and a few things learned, because for twenty minutes they were hungry, afraid, relieved, curious and in company as one (D1, D2).

## Pillars

Use these to judge any feature. If a feature fails one, cut it or change it.

1. **You are the shrimp.** Camera, sound and interface express what the shrimp senses and feels. No health bar, no meters.
2. **Calm first.** Fear exists and chases happen, but they are short and always resolve into safety.
3. **True to the animal.** Every behaviour in the game is one real shrimp show, simplified (D7).
4. **No animal is consumed.** See hard rules.
5. **Never preach.** The game shows and lets the player feel. Facts wait in an optional journal (D3).
6. **Something to do.** Small challenges give players a reason to explore, taste, meet and escape (D18).

## Hard rules

These are not open for tuning.

- No shrimp, no other animal and no animal product is ever eaten, by any character, in play, images, text or sound.
- The game shows shrimp eating only algae and plant matter. It never states that this is all they eat (D7, D8).
- No death and no injury is shown. No violence toward any animal.
- The player is never blamed or shamed, and never told what to do outside the game.
- Every fact in the journal has a source in [07-science-and-message.md](07-science-and-message.md).
- Every asset is original, CC0 or CC BY, and listed in `CREDITS.md`. No AI-generated music (D26, D27).

## Audience and platform

- General public, teens and adults, including people who rarely play games (D1).
- Runs in a desktop browser from a link. Keyboard only in the first version (D20).
- French and English (D28).

## The player shrimp (D6)

A whiteleg shrimp (*Penaeus vannamei*), native to the eastern Pacific coast from Mexico to northern Peru.

- It is the shrimp most people have eaten, so the empathy lands on the right animal.
- Its real life is a journey. Adults spawn in the open ocean. Larvae drift to the coast. The young grow up in estuaries, lagoons and mangroves, then return to sea. That gives the game its chapters.

## View (D16)

Top-down. The player looks straight down through the water onto the bed. The shrimp is seen from above and turns freely to face where it goes. Things above it, such as leaves, a bird, a hull or a net, show as shadows crossing the ground.

## Structure (D15, D11)

One life in six short chapters, then a free life with no end.

| # | Chapter | Minutes | Life stage | New for the player |
|---|---------|---------|------------|--------------------|
| 0 | Drift | 1 to 2 | Larva | Steer. Eat drifting microalgae. |
| 1 | Mangrove | 6 | Juvenile | Walk, graze, sense, hide. First fright. First meeting. First molt. |
| 2 | Lagoon | 5 | Sub-adult | Day and night. Burrow. Companions and bonds. A fish that chases. |
| 3 | Channel | 4 | Sub-adult | Travel seaward with the tide at night, together. |
| 4 | The shadow | 2 | Sub-adult | An engine, a net. Get out from under it (D4). |
| 5 | Open sea | 3 | Adult | Quiet. Spawning. The loop closes. |
| | Free life | no limit | Next generation | The whole coast, open. Challenges and journal to finish. |

### Beats

**0. Drift.** Dark water, then light. You are a speck among specks, carried by the tide. Green motes glow, and touching one feels good. The coast rises ahead as a brown haze.

**1. Mangrove.** Tea-coloured water over mud and fallen leaves, roots everywhere. You settle and learn to walk and graze the algae film on a root. Pressing Sense fills the water with scent trails. A wide shadow slides across the ground: a wading bird. You tuck under the roots and the fear drains away. Another young shrimp watches from a leaf. You touch antennae. Your shell begins to feel tight. You find shelter and molt, soft and slow for a while, then larger.

**2. Lagoon.** Open sand and seagrass. By day the light is bright and it is safer to burrow. At dusk the shoal comes out to graze. Night brings glowing plankton. You get to know Brise, Nacre and Écume. A green turtle grazes the seagrass and ignores you. A pistol shrimp keeps house with a goby. At dawn a hunting fish comes through, and this time it chases.

**3. Channel.** The tide pulls seaward and the shoal goes with it under moonlight. Stronger current, a predator that follows, companions that warn each other. Tail flips matter here.

**4. The shadow.** A low throb in the water. A long dark hull crosses above, and behind it a grid of shadow sweeps the bed. Everyone scatters. You dart out from under it, or press into a crevice until it has gone. The shoal regroups. No one is taken. Nothing is explained.

**5. Open sea.** Deep blue, slow music, a full-grown body that swims with ease. Spawning is shown as a pale cloud of eggs rising in the dark (D12). The view follows one egg as it hatches and begins to drift toward the coast.

**Free life.** There is no closing card (D3). The drifting larva reaches the mangrove and the player is that shrimp: the next generation. Every area is now open with no story steps. The journal, challenges and achievements carry over. When this shrimp reaches the open sea and spawns, the cycle repeats and the generation count goes up (D11, proposed form).

## Core loop

Minute to minute, in any order the player likes:

1. Sense the water.
2. Travel toward something: food, a companion, an unknown shape, a challenge.
3. Graze and enjoy it.
4. Find something new. The journal fills.
5. Meet or follow another shrimp.
6. Something startles you, or hunts you. Hide or dart away.
7. Calm returns. Rest.

## Feelings (D17)

The shrimp has five feelings: hunger, fear, comfort, curiosity and company. There are no bars. The player reads them from body language, sound, light and a sparse line of narration.

They must be unmistakable. Each mood has its own posture, its own music state and its own screen treatment, and the first time each one occurs the narration names it once. Detail in [02-mechanics.md](02-mechanics.md).

Other shrimp run the same feelings model as the player. They visibly hesitate, startle, relax and seek each other out. This is how the game says "they feel too" without saying it.

## Challenges and achievements (D18)

Players get things to do, and a place to see what they have done.

- **Story steps.** Two to four per chapter. They move the life forward: eat your fill, meet another shrimp, get away from the bird, molt.
- **Challenges ("Défis").** Four to six optional ones per chapter: find something, taste something, reach a place, meet someone, watch a creature, escape cleanly. They are worded as things a shrimp does, not as tasks.
- **Achievements ("Succès").** About twenty across the whole game, including the free life.

Full lists in [02-mechanics.md](02-mechanics.md).

## Other shrimp (D13)

A shoal, with three individuals the player comes to recognise by markings and temperament.

| Name | Marking | Temperament |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Brise | Nicked left antenna | Bold. First to explore, last to hide. |
| Nacre | Pale tail fan | Cautious. Spots danger early. |
| Écume | Smaller, faster | Curious. Wanders off, comes back. |

The player's shrimp has no name. It is "you".

**Speech.** Recommended: they do not speak. The narration tells the player how each one seems ("Nacre has stopped. Something is wrong."), and their bodies do the rest. Text speech would turn them into cartoon people, which works against "only behaviours real shrimp show" and against the point that real shrimp, as they are, feel. This is still open (D13).

Bonds grow from greeting, grazing side by side, sheltering together and fleeing together. Bonded companions follow when called, warn of danger, stay close during a molt and lead the player to food.

## Other species (D14)

- A pistol shrimp and the goby that shares its burrow. A real partnership between two species. The player can watch and be tolerated.
- A green turtle grazing seagrass. Calm, huge, harmless.
- Fiddler crabs, snails and small schooling fish as background life.
- Predators: a wading bird in the mangrove, a hunting fish in the lagoon and channel. They chase. They never catch anyone on screen (D9).

## Progression

Growth happens through molting, as in life. The shrimp gets larger and more capable, the world gets wider, and the group gets closer. There are no points, levels or upgrades to buy.

## The journal, "Le carnet" (D18, D3)

Opened with its own key or from the pause menu. Five pages:

- **Discoveries.** Foods, places and creatures met.
- **Companions.** One page per friend, filling in as the bond grows.
- **What shrimp feel.** Short cards linking something the player just did to what researchers have observed. One or two sentences and a source.
- **Challenges.** By chapter, with progress.
- **Achievements.**

The journal is the only place facts are stated. Reading it is optional.

## About page (D5)

Reached from the title screen. It holds the sources for the journal, the credits and the note on visit counting. The game is a personal project. It names no organisation and links to no campaign.

## Tension and failure (D9, D10)

Predators appear and chase. The player gets away most of the time, because warnings are clear, shelters are close and three tail flips outrun anything.

If cornered: the screen blurs, the shrimp darts off on its own, and the player wakes in the nearest shelter, shaken, with nothing lost.

A "gentle mode" in settings removes chases and keeps shadows and sounds.

## What this game is not

Not a survival game. No combat, no crafting, no inventory, no farm management, no talking mascots, no timers, no jump scares, no message screens.

## How we know it works

- At least 7 of 10 playtesters reach the open sea.
- Asked "what is a shrimp like?" afterwards, they use feeling words.
- They can name one thing shrimp do that they did not know.
- Nobody reports feeling lectured.
